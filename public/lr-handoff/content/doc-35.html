[LlamaLend]: syrupUSDC Collateral Onboarding Assessment

Executive Summary

syrupUSDC is a yield-bearing stablecoin (ERC-4626) backed by Maple Direct's overcollateralized institutional loan book. This assessment evaluates its suitability as collateral for LlamaLend across asset fundamentals, market liquidity, technological, and counterparty risk.

The material difference for LlamaLend is the oracle-to-liquidation path. Unlike hard-threshold lending markets, LlamaLend liquidates continuously through LLAMMA, which requires the collateral to be sold into on-chain liquidity as the oracle price falls. syrupUSDC's value is a function of an off-chain loan book whose impairments are marked discretely on-chain. This creates a specific, real risk surface — the marking-lag window and thin secondary exit liquidity — that does not exist for simple liquid collateral. We document this in a dedicated section below (Section 4a) and treat it as the binding consideration for parameterization.

The single largest driver of potential value loss is loan-book concentration: the largest single loan in the syrupUSDC pool is $200M (~15.5% of the pool's ~$1.29B open-term book), the most concentrated borrower holds ~$262M (~20.4%), and the top three borrowers hold ~49% of pool principal; three borrowers also operate across both Maple pools, together ~29% of combined principal (all API-verified, June 17, 2026). A default on the largest loan would mark syrupUSDC's NAV down in a single block. This is the basis for the LTV methodology to be developed separately: the market's maximum LTV must sit below the value that survives a single-largest-loan default, otherwise an impairment instantly produces bad debt for crvUSD lenders.

1. Asset Fundamental Characteristics

1.1 Asset

syrupUSDC is a tokenized note over a loan book backed by collateralized liquid digital assets. It is best characterized as a concentrated yield-bearing stablecoin; structurally it is an underlying and segregated pool of USDC issued to independent, KYC'd institutional borrowers underwritten by Maple Direct. The syrupUSDC ERC-4626 vault represents the sum of all USDC deposited into the pool. A portion of backing is held as readily available redemption liquidity, deployed across DeFi venues; the remainder is lent out as Open Term Loans. Yield is therefore derived from both active loans and DeFi yield on the liquidity buffer.

The current Maple Direct model is overcollateralized, crypto-asset-backed lending — not unsecured commercial credit (the latter describes Maple's earlier, deprecated 2021–22 model). Permissioned institutional borrowers (trading firms, market makers, funds) draw USDC and pledge liquid crypto collateral (BTC, ETH, XRP, etc.) worth more than the loan, maintained above 100% collateralization at all times. Borrowers complete KYC/AML and underwriting that assesses balance-sheet strength and the operational ability to meet margin calls quickly in falling markets. Collateral is held in institutional custody (e.g. Anchorage, BitGo, Zodia), with on-chain custody addresses published for verification. Critically, the USDC disbursement and loan accounting are on-chain (the loan-manager contracts), while collateral custody, margin-call cure, and liquidation are executed off-chain / OTC — the on-chain/off-chain split that underlies the marking-lag risk in Section 4a.

A portion of pool assets is held as recallable, multi-chain redemption liquidity (Ethereum, BNB, Solana, Base, Arbitrum, Plasma, Mantle). Over the trailing three months, Maple's annualized returns declined to ~4–5%, down from the 6–9% range observed in mid-2025.

The pool's gross open-term loan book stands at ~$1.29B across 40 active loans with zero impaired (API-verified, June 17, 2026); this gross figure includes treasury/AMM and basis-trade allocations alongside secured credit. Collateral composition is disclosed off-chain by Maple and is not on-chain/API-verifiable: it has historically been BTC-dominated (~75% of secured collateral) with XRP the next sleeve, and collateralization ratios vary by asset (volatile collateral such as HYPE held at higher ratios, ~1.76x; stablecoin/treasury near 1.00x). See Appendix A for the live breakdown.

Relevance to LlamaLend: syrupUSDC's redemption value is the loan book's value. The composition above — concentrated, BTC-dominated, off-chain-collateralized — is what determines how much value syrupUSDC can lose in a default, and is carried forward into the Maple-default risk surface (Section 4a) and the LTV methodology.

1.1.1 Asset Composition and Yield Sources

syrupUSDC's backing is not exclusively secured credit. Pool assets (totalAssets ≈ $1.305B on-chain at the time of writing) consist of the gross open-term book (~$1.29B, API-verified) plus a small residual cash buffer. Critically, the supporting strategies below are themselves booked inside that gross open-term figure rather than as a separate buffer, so the gross number is not all secured credit. Per Maple's disclosures, the declared scope is:

  1. Overcollateralized institutional lending (primary). syrupUSDC blends two secured sleeves:

    • Blue Chip Secured — BTC/ETH collateral, lower yield, tight collateral ratios (~1.0–1.1x).

    • High Yield Secured — more volatile collateral (XRP, HYPE, SOL, …), higher yield, higher required collateral ratios (e.g. HYPE at 1.76x).

  2. Futures basis trading — cash-and-carry capturing BTC futures term-structure spreads.

  3. DeFi liquidity provision — idle USDC deployed into DeFi protocols (Aave, Fluid, etc.) to support the instant-redemption buffer.

  4. T-Bills — short-dated treasury exposure.

This means a portion of syrupUSDC's value depends on strategies beyond the secured loan book; the loan book is roughly half of total pool assets, the remainder being buffer/treasury operations. The full live book is detailed in Appendix A.

Declared collateral and loan criteria. Collateral is evaluated for liquidity, historical volatility, and technical security; assets without acceptable liquidity are ineligible. Concentration limits are applied so Maple is never too large a share of any single market (to bound liquidation slippage), more volatile collateral requires higher collateral-to-loan ratios, and liquidation levels are set conservatively above 100% collateralization. Each loan carries its own Initial / Margin-Call / Liquidation levels; a breach of the margin-call level triggers a 24-hour cure window before OTC liquidation under the borrower's legal agreement. Maple reports zero losses to date. These are operational policies and track record, not protocol-enforced guarantees — the distinction the LTV methodology must price.

Relevance to LlamaLend: syrupUSDC's redemption value is the loan book's value. The composition above — concentrated, BTC-dominated, off-chain-collateralized — is what determines how much value syrupUSDC can lose in a default, and is carried forward into the Maple-default risk surface (Section 4a) and the LTV methodology.

1.2 Architecture

Fixed-rate loan underwriting and liquidity management are operated on Ethereum by Maple. The token is an immutable ERC-20 / ERC-4626 vault; redemptions are processed through a withdrawal queue.

Redemption performance has improved over time and is relevant to liquidation viability (see Section 2.1 and Section 4a):

  • Withdrawal-queue median time-to-process: 3.6 minutes; size-weighted average 1.21h.

  • 91.4% of redemptions (90.9% of volume) settle within the hour.

  • Tail risk concentrates in 320 events exceeding 24 hours (~65.4M USDC), worst case 124.7h. These are typically smaller, older requests rather than recent large ones.

  • Weekly size-weighted averages show only 9 weeks ever crossed the 24-hour threshold, all in the early operating period; recent weeks are near zero, indicating the pool is now sized to absorb redemptions as they arrive.

Relevance to LlamaLend: primary redemption (mint/redeem at NAV) is one of the two exit paths a liquidator/arbitrageur has when unwinding soft-liquidated syrupUSDC. Fast redemption helps in normal conditions; however, redemption is at the current NAV and is subject to the available liquidity buffer — both of which degrade precisely in the default scenario that matters (Section 4a).

2. Market Risk

2.1 Liquidity

Swap liquidity for syrupUSDC within a 7.5% price impact stands at 12.35M (~$14.35M), up from ~10.28M ($11.41M) over recent months. Simulated routes indicate most swaps within this range are facilitated via Uniswap and Fluid pools, so syrupUSDC has deep primary liquidity to cover liquidations in normal conditions.

2.1.1 Liquidity Venue Concentration

Ethereum Mainnet liquidity is predominantly in the Uniswap V4 syrupUSDC/USDC pool, with additional depth on Fluid.

2.1.2 DEX LP Concentration

A Maple-controlled wallet remains the majority liquidity provider, supplying ~$15M in USDC on Uniswap V4 via Arrakis. Secondary liquidity is therefore reliant on a single venue and a single, protocol-aligned source.

Relevance to LlamaLend: this is the most important market-risk caveat for an LLAMMA market. Soft-liquidation depends on arbitrageurs buying syrupUSDC out of the AMM and offloading it on-chain. The bulk of executable exit liquidity is Maple-provided and concentrated in one pool; under a Maple-default scenario this same liquidity can widen or be withdrawn (see Section 4a, T3). Stressed executable liquidity — not headline $14.35M spot depth — is the correct benchmark for liquidation-discount and exposure sizing.

2.2 Volatility

The syrupUSDC/USDC conversion rate on Uniswap V4 tracks the internal exchange rate closely, trading at a mean discount of ~9 bps. The largest absolute secondary-market deviation observed was 22 bps (April 20, 2026, during the Kelp exploit event), which mean-reverted. There has been no sustained (≥2% for ≥2 days) deviation from the internal rate.

2.3 Exchanges

syrupUSDC is exclusively traded on DEXs and is not currently listed on any centralized exchange. There is no CEX exit path for liquidators.

2.4 Growth

Supply has contracted from its March 2026 peak but DeFi utilization remains high. Growth is demand-driven (no emissions-based incentives sustaining the peg); the asset trades on its yield and redemption guarantees rather than mercenary liquidity.

3.3 Price Feed Risk

The pricing path is the central technological consideration for the LlamaLend market.

Proposed oracle construction. The market prices the collateral (syrupUSDC) against the debt asset (crvUSD) as the product of two legs:

p(syrupUSDC / crvUSD) = convertToExitAssets(syrupUSDC → USDC)  ×  EMA( USDC/crvUSD pool price )

  • Leg 1 — price-per-share, convertToExitAssets. This converts one syrupUSDC into its USDC redemption value. The correct read is convertToExitAssets (not convertToAssets): only convertToExitAssets reflects unrealizedLosses, i.e. it steps down immediately when a loan is impaired, whereas convertToAssets ignores impairment and would keep printing full NAV. Reading the exit rate is what allows the oracle to recognize a marked impairment in the same block. This leg is not smoothed — smoothing it would delay recognition of a downward mark, which is exactly the move the market must react to.

  • Leg 2 — USDC/crvUSD pool price with EMA. This converts the USDC value into crvUSD terms using the on-chain USDC/crvUSD Curve pool price, EMA-smoothed. Smoothing this leg dampens transient pool noise and manipulation of the USDC/crvUSD quote without blunting the impairment signal, which lives in Leg 1. Pricing USDC against crvUSD via the live pool (rather than assuming 1 USDC = 1 crvUSD) means a USDC depeg relative to crvUSD is captured directly by the oracle.

Note that Maple internally hardcodes USDC to $1 inside its UsdOracle (referenced from the Maple Global contract). This is internal to Maple's own loan-book accounting and is distinct from the LlamaLend oracle above; since USDC is Maple's loan asset, the static $1 assumption only affects Maple's NAV on an upward USDC depeg (debt undervalued), historically rare and short-lived given active arbitrage — low in practice.

Key oracle limitation (carried into Section 4a). Leg 1 only moves when Maple marks an impairment on-chain. It is blind to off-chain credit deterioration and to DEX repricing in the interval between a loan going bad and the delegate calling impairLoan(). The exchange rate can be rate-limited on the upside (to resist redemption-rate manipulation) but cannot anticipate a downside mark, and — per above — Leg 1 is deliberately left unsmoothed so the mark is recognized promptly. The remaining tunable is the EMA window on Leg 2 and LLAMMA's own Texp/limit_p_o, which are Section 5 calibration decisions.

Manipulation resistance on the PPS leg. Because convertToExitAssets is a vault price-per-share read, it is structurally exposed to the single-block PPS-inflation class of attack seen in the sDOLA-long2 incident, where a permissionless donation to the underlying vault was used to atomically spike the oracle and force liquidations. That vector is dormant for syrupUSDC today — its ~1.1B supply is externally held and cannot be cornered, redemptions are queued rather than atomic, and donating against a ~$1.3B asset base to move PPS by any meaningful amount is prohibitively expensive — but the protection is purely economic and could erode if supply contracted or a single market came to hold a large share of it. We therefore convert this into a structural guarantee with two complementary controls on the PPS leg: (1) an upward rate-limit / cap on convertToExitAssets, so the oracle cannot accept an instantaneous jump in share price regardless of cause; and (2) a very short EMA over the read. Even a short window makes single-block manipulation infeasible — a one-block donation contributes only ≈ Δt/Texp of weight (on the order of a percent or two), so an attacker would have to sustain the donation across many blocks, which de-atomizes the attack, removes flash-loan financing, and exposes the donated capital to arbitrage. The window is kept deliberately short so that downside recognition of a genuine Maple impairment is delayed only by minutes — negligible against the hours-scale, discretionary marking lag at T0 (Section 4a) that dominates syrupUSDC's actual risk.

3.4 Dependency Risk

Loan Collateral

syrupUSDC's effective collateral set is concentrated around Bitcoin and assets native to secondary networks — XRP Ledger and Hyperliquid. These operate on chains with distinct security assumptions and network-specific market dynamics, and their liquidation is conducted off-chain/OTC by Maple. This concentration is the dependency that propagates into syrupUSDC's redemption value during a default (Section 4a).

Infrastructure

The mainnet market's primary external dependency is Maple's own loan-management and impairment machinery. (The Base instance additionally depends on Chainlink CCIP; out of scope for this mainnet assessment.)

4a. The Maple-Default Risk Surface (LlamaLend-specific)

This is the risk that most distinguishes a syrupUSDC LlamaLend market from one collateralized by a simple liquid asset, and the one that should govern parameterization. syrupUSDC's value is a function of an off-chain loan book whose losses are recognized on-chain only when Maple marks them. In a continuous-liquidation system like LLAMMA, the interaction between discrete NAV marks and thin secondary exit liquidity defines the bad-debt risk.

Why a default loses value at all

The loans are originated off-chain but are collateralized (BTC, XRP, ETH, custodied). When a borrower defaults, Maple liquidates that collateral OTC and applies proceeds to principal. The loss to the pool = principal − recovery from the collateral sale. That loss is booked as unrealizedLosses/realizedLosses, and syrupUSDC NAV drops by loss / totalAssets in a single block:

convertToExitAssets = (totalAssets − unrealizedLosses) / totalSupply

Recovery is not automatically 100%: the largest loan carries a permissive 95.2% liquidation level, BTC can gap through that level in a thin OTC window, and concentration means a correlated move can impair several loans at once. The size of this loss is the subject of the LTV methodology (to be developed separately).

Concentration — the magnitude driver

Observed loan-book composition:

  • Largest single loan: $200M (~15.5% of the pool's ~$1.29B book) — BTC-collateralized at a permissive 95.2% liquidation level per Maple's off-chain disclosures, a wide margin-call-to-liquidation band relative to peers.

  • Most concentrated borrower: ~$262M (~20.4% of the pool) across two loans; the top three borrowers hold ~49% of pool principal (API-verified).

  • The two Maple pools are not independent risk silos: three borrowers operate across both pools and together hold ~$486M (~29% of combined principal); the largest cross-pool borrower holds ~$235M ($60M in syrupUSDC + $175M in syrupUSDT, ~13.9% of combined) — cross-pool overlap verified via borrower addresses in the API. A single default hits syrupUSDC and syrupUSDT lenders concurrently.

  • Margin calls have historically been cured within hours (observed instances include a $200M and a $30M loan both cured within 48h via off-chain collateral top-ups), but cure is an off-chain, discretionary process — not a protocol guarantee.

Liquidation processing (off-chain → on-chain)

Maple's procedure is: monitor → margin call (12–24h cure window) → OTC liquidation → and only then reflect any shortfall on-chain. Impairment requires Investment Committee sign-off + 3-of-5 multisig and books the residual as unrealizedLosses; the terminal default step books realizedLosses and permanently lowers the exchange rate. A legal default alone does not automatically move the on-chain rate — the on-chain mark is a discretionary, multi-step action.

Timeline of a default through LLAMMA

T0 — Loan goes bad, before Maple acts. On-chain state unchanged: issuanceRate still running, unrealizedLosses still 0, convertToExitAssets still prints full NAV. Off-chain reality is already impaired — informed traders see the missed payment and begin dumping syrupUSDC on Uniswap; DEX price drops below NAV. The oracle is blind to this. This marking-lag window — where NAV does not match the real redemption price — is the single most dangerous moment, because LLAMMA will not begin soft-liquidating a position that the oracle still believes is healthy.

T1 — Delegate calls impairLoan() (discretionary timing). The loan's issuanceRate is pulled, principal + accrued interest is booked into unrealizedLosses, and NAV drops in the same block by the impaired fraction. This is exactly why the oracle must read convertToExitAssets (which captures unrealizedLosses) and not convertToAssets (which would not move).

T2 — LLAMMA digests the drop. The oracle price p_o falls X% in one block. LLAMMA's limit_p_o caps how fast its internal price can track the external oracle per block, spreading the move over several blocks rather than gapping instantly. This is AMM self-protection against oracle manipulation; it incidentally cushions the discontinuity. Soft-liquidation begins as p_o falls.

T3 — Liquidation execution (the real test). Soft-liquidation must find buyers. Arbitrageurs buy syrupUSDC out of the AMM only if they can offload it downstream, and post-default they have two degraded options: (1) redeem at Maple — now queued/congested, at the new lower NAV, against a finite (~$0.6M instant) buffer; or (2) sell on Uniswap — a ~$15M, single-source pool, already trading below NAV. Arbitrageurs will not fill at the oracle's NAV; they wait for the AMM price to drop further to clear the market discount. Soft-liquidation becomes sluggish or partial, positions linger in syrupUSDC, and if the drop exceeds the borrower's LTV buffer the position goes underwater → bad debt to crvUSD lenders.


Parametrization

Because an impairment marks NAV down in one block and exit liquidity is thin and concentrated, the market's maximum LTV must sit below the value that survives a single-largest-loan default. If max LTV exceeds that, the impairment instantly produces bad debt rather than a soft-liquidatable position. Quantifying that ceiling — the loss-given-default of the largest loan as a fraction of NAV, plus an execution buffer, with Maple default probability sizing the buffer — is the subject of the LTV methodology to be developed in a follow-up.

5.3 Exposure Limits

The syrupUSDC/crvUSD is treated as a fresh market, the method does not rely on observed borrower positions. Instead, it converts stressed executable liquidation capacity into a borrow-cap estimate using an assumed borrower LTV, a price-shock scenario, and a residual hard-liquidation pressure model.

The liquidity-capacity input is based on the syrupUSDC → crvUSD route. Route capacities are not summed, because different routes may share the same underlying liquidity. At a maximum accepted liquidation slippage of 0.6%, the observed route capacity was 8.28m syrupUSDC. This was divided by a 1.5× liquidity-contraction factor, giving a stressed executable capacity of 5.52m syrupUSDC.

The market parameters imply a maximum LTV of:

Lmax​=1−loan discount−2AN​

Using a loan discount of 1.8%, (A = 312), and (N = 4), this gives:

Lmax​=97.559

The hard-liquidation LTV proxy is:

Lhard​=1−liquidation discount=99.4

The borrower LTV assumption was set equal to maximum LTV. This is conservative but appropriate for this use case, because these markets are expected to be primarily used for looping strategies, where borrowers are likely to operate close to the maximum allowed LTV.

The empirical shock input was estimated from the syrupUSDC/crvUSD price feed using a 60-minute downside window-shock method. The shock extraction used downside direction, a top-50 event selection, a 60-minute declustering window, a 1.5× trigger multiplier, and selected the mean of the top declustered downside events. This produced an empirical price shock of:

sempirical​=0.2007

The top-event distribution was relatively mild: the top-50 median shock was 0.1316%, the mean was 0.2007%, the p90 was 0.3123%, and the maximum selected event was 1.0544%.

This empirical shock is not a simple rolling ES estimate. It is an event-based downside stress estimate designed to avoid overweighting many overlapping observations from the same price episode. The declustering step is important because rolling 60-minute returns from 1-minute data are highly overlapping; without declustering, a single depeg episode can generate many near-duplicate observations and distort the tail estimate.

However, the empirical shock alone is below the model-implied hard-liquidation trigger:

strigger​=1−Lhard​Lmax​​=1.8521

Since the empirical shock of 0.2007% does not breach this threshold, it does not create hard-liquidation pressure under the buffer model. To avoid falling back to a full-pressure assumption while still producing a binding cap-sizing scenario, the structural-trigger policy was used. This sets the sizing shock equal to the trigger shock plus a 25 bps margin:

ssizing​=strigger​+0.25

This produces a shocked price of 1.1456 and a stressed LTV of 99.65%. Under this scenario, the model estimates that 11.89% of the assumed borrower exposure becomes residual hard-liquidation pressure.

Soft-liquidation efficiency was set to 0%. This means the model does not credit soft liquidation with reducing hard-liquidation pressure over the short stress horizon. This is intentionally conservative: for a short 60-minute stress window, the analysis assumes that soft-liquidation execution may not reliably absorb the adverse move before hard-liquidation pressure has to be cleared through external syrupUSDC/crvUSD liquidity.

The resulting structural-trigger buffer-adjusted borrow cap is:

51.86m

The full-pressure cap represents the conservative bound obtained by assuming that the full residual borrower exposure must be liquidated through external liquidity, without applying the LTV-buffer adjustment that reduces pressure when only a fraction of positions breaches the hard-liquidation threshold. It is reported as a conservative lower-bound diagnostic:

6.17m

This result should be interpreted as a structural-trigger buffer-adjusted cap, not as a full-pressure liquidation cap. The empirical shock was non-binding; the final sizing scenario intentionally uses the model-implied liquidation trigger plus margin to ensure that the cap reflects a buffer-breach stress without defaulting to the overly conservative full-pressure assumption. The large gap between the buffer-adjusted cap and the full-pressure cap is driven by the fact that, under the structural-trigger scenario, only 11.89% of assumed borrower exposure is modeled as residual hard-liquidation pressure.

Appendix B — LTV Methodology

This appendix defines how the market's maximum LTV is bounded by the worst single-borrower default net of collateral recovery, then applies it to the live syrupUSDC book (June 2026). All inputs are sourced live (on-chain + Maple's public GraphQL API).

B.1 Principle

syrupUSDC is unusual in that its collateral value can step down discretely when Maple marks an impairment (Section 4a), rather than drifting continuously. The binding safety condition is therefore:

The market's maximum LTV must sit below the syrupUSDC value that survives the single most damaging borrower default. If max LTV exceeds it, the impairment mark instantly pushes high-LTV positions underwater — producing bad debt for crvUSD lenders before LLAMMA can soft-liquidate.

Formally:

max_LTV≤1−LNAV​−Bexec​

  • LNAV​ — the one-block NAV drop from the worst single-borrower default, net of recovery from seized collateral, as a fraction of pool assets.

  • Bexec​ — execution buffer: extra margin because, after the NAV mark, the crvUSD position must still be unwound through thin, single-source syrupUSDC liquidity (Section 2.1, stage T3).

Maple's default probability does not move the ceiling; it sizes Bexec​ and selects how adverse a recovery haircut to assume (deterministic ceiling + probability-sized buffer).

B.3 Step 1 — Identify the binding default scenario

Default is a borrower-level event: a defaulting borrower defaults on all its loans simultaneously, and every loan's collateral is seized. The NAV-loss contribution of a borrower is therefore the sum of the per-loan losses across all of that borrower's loans:

lossb​=i∈loans(b)∑​max(0,principali​−recoveryi​)

Each loan's collateral covers only that loan — excess collateral on one loan is not netted against a shortfall on another (collateral is posted per-loan; this no-netting treatment is the conservative, pool-protective assumption). The binding borrower is argmaxb​lossb​. Borrowers are ranked by this summed loss, not by raw exposure.

The raw-largest borrower is not the binding one. The largest syrupUSDC borrower (~$242M, across 3 loans) is a PYUSD/USTB treasury allocation (cash-equivalent, near-zero default loss). The binding borrower holds a single $200M BTC loan — not the largest exposure, but the thinnest cushion (liquidation level 1.05x, i.e. only ~5% over principal at the trigger; every other BTC loan sits at 1.10–1.20x). The second-ranked borrower demonstrates the aggregation: it holds three loans (XRP $109M + BTC $10M) whose losses are summed (Step B.7).

Cross-pool note: a cross-pool borrower's default affects syrupUSDC NAV only through its syrupUSDC slice. The XRP-heavy borrower (Step 4) is cross-pool ($109M XRP + BTC in syrupUSDC, plus a syrupUSDT slice), so its default would hit both pools — relevant to systemic, not single-pool, NAV.

B.4 Step 2 — Seizable collateral and recovery

Assume a defaulting loan has ridden down to its liquidation trigger (collateral ≈ principal × liqLevel), and the off-chain sale then realizes a haircut h (gap during the marking-lag window + OTC slippage). Per-loan loss:

lossi​=principali​×max(0,1−liqLeveli​(1−hi​))

This is conservative: it ignores today's much healthier collateral (the $200M loan currently sits at ~1.56x vs its 1.05x trigger), because a default stress is precisely the path where collateral erodes to the trigger before sale.

B.8 Limitation: collateral correlation (single-borrower vs systemic stress)

This methodology measures the single most damaging borrower default in isolation. That is the right binding constraint for setting an LTV floor, but it understates tail risk, because the loan book's collateral is highly correlated: BTC, cbBTC, ETH and XRP all sell off together in a broad crypto crash. In such an event:

  • Most high-LTV / thin-cushion loans would breach their liquidation levels at roughly the same time, so realized loss would be the aggregate across many loans — not one $200M borrower.

  • OTC and on-chain exit liquidity is shared and finite; when every position liquidates at once, executable haircuts rise above the per-asset figures in B.4 (the haircut should itself become a function of aggregate liquidation volume, not a fixed per-collateral constant).

  • The cross-pool overlap (~29% of combined principal, Appendix A) means syrupUSDC and syrupUSDT liquidate into the same stressed venues simultaneously.

This correlation property is acknowledged but deliberately not modeled here — the single-borrower deterministic ceiling is the tractable, conservative constraint that feeds loan_discount. A more refined approach would estimate the value-at-risk of the whole loan book under multiple joint stress scenarios (e.g. correlated BTC/alt drawdowns of varying severity), each weighted by likelihood, producing a portfolio loss distribution rather than a single point — with correlated liquidation timing, liquidity-dependent haircuts, and cross-pool effects all captured. The single-borrower result should therefore be read as a lower bound on systemic tail loss, and the chosen Bexec​ / confidence level should leave headroom for it.

B.9 Refresh cadence

These results are a point-in-time snapshot; the book changes continuously. Re-run when exposures, the binding borrower, or its collateral mix change materially. The full pipeline is reproducible from public data: openTermLoans(where:{state:Active}) for exposure + collateral + liquidation levels, an external spot price for current-health context, and totalAssets() on-chain — no access-gated source required. The cheapest monitoring trigger is isImpaired and the binding loan's exposure/liquidation level.
